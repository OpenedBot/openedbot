openedbot orchestrates your bots. It does not administer your server. This page states what is guaranteed to you, and what is not. The tool's commands are in [edge-setup](/docs/edge-setup).

## What stays with you

- The software that runs your bots (Hermes).
- Your machine's own secrets: they never leave it.
- The keys to your AI services. Keys you enter in the console are passed to your machine without being stored or logged by openedbot.
- Your bots' files, memory and work history on the machine.

You open no port on your router and you expose no address of your machine.

## What openedbot never does

- It never opens a session on your machine: no SSH, no terminal.
- It runs no free-form command and does not read the contents of your disk.
- It does not update your machine for you.
- It restarts Hermes only when you ask for it in the console.

It does only what the console needs: run your bots, show their status and configure their services and functions.

## Link and remove a machine

- The ticket works only once and expires after five minutes.
- You can remove a linked machine from the console at any time: it then loses access to your workspace.
- Traffic between your machine and openedbot is encrypted in transit.

## What this page does not guarantee

| Risk | Who handles it |
|---|---|
| Someone has root on your machine | You. Root sees everything on the machine, keys included. |
| VPS provider snapshot or console | The provider. `edge-setup` does not encrypt the disk. |
| A page or file read by the bot (injection) | Approvals in the console. The bot can reach the Internet freely. |
| The model provider (Anthropic, etc.) | It receives the messages the bot sends to it. |
| `up` without hardening options | No firewall, no hardened SSH, no fail2ban. See [edge-setup](/docs/edge-setup). |

## Report a vulnerability

Do not publish an exploitable vulnerability; report it privately through
GitHub Security Advisories
([Report a vulnerability](https://github.com/OpenedBot/openedbot/security/advisories/new)),
with the version (`edge-setup version`) and the impact. Never send a ticket,
a password or a key.

Each `edge-setup` release is signed by openedbot's release workflow on
GitHub, for the tag of its version, and the signature is logged in Sigstore's
public transparency log. Installation and updates verify that signature, then
the binary's SHA-256 checksum, before installing anything: nothing is installed
if either check fails, and an unsigned release is refused. See
[edge-setup](/docs/edge-setup).
